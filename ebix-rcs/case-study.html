The Challenge

A Certificate of Insurance Isn't the Whole Story

For years, construction project managers have relied on certificates of insurance (COIs) to verify that subcontractors carry the right coverage. On the surface, the system works: a subcontractor submits a COI showing the required limits, the box gets checked, and work begins.

But buried in the Schedules of Endorsements (SOEs) attached to those same policies are exclusions that can silently invalidate coverage for entire categories of work — residential construction, exterior insulation and finish systems (EIFS), roofing, earthwork, and more. These trade-specific exclusions don't appear on the certificate face. They surface only in the claims process, long after the work is done.

The discovery that changed the approach: A subcontractor's policy showed adequate limits, but their SOE contained an exclusion for residential work. The exposure wasn't discovered until a claim was filed — at which point, coverage was denied. The general contractor absorbed the loss directly.

This wasn't an isolated incident. It was systematic. And it was invisible to the existing review process.

The Problem

Why Traditional Compliance Reviews Miss the Risk

Standard COI collection processes are designed to verify that coverage exists — not to analyze what that coverage actually covers. Most firms never receive SOEs at all, and when they do, the documents are dense, inconsistently formatted, and require deep insurance expertise to interpret.

The resulting blind spots are predictable: trade-specific exclusions go unchecked because reviewers don't know to look for them; endorsements arrive on different schedules than certificates; manual tracking systems can't correlate trade scope with exclusion language at scale; and when exceptions do get flagged, there's no structured workflow to resolve them before work begins.

For a firm managing hundreds of active subcontractors across multiple simultaneous projects, the risk compounds quickly — and quietly.

The Solution

Trade-Aware Compliance Built for How Construction Actually Works

Ebix RCS was configured with custom rules-based logic that understood not just what coverage a subcontractor carried, but whether that coverage remained intact given the specific trades they were engaged to perform.

Trade-Specific Exclusion Logic
Custom rules mapped each subcontractor's trade scope against known exclusion categories — automatically flagging any SOE language that could invalidate coverage for the work they were hired to perform.
Automated SOE Collection and Validation
The system required SOE submission alongside standard COIs — and validated that submissions were complete, current, and cross-referenced against the rules before any subcontractor could be cleared to work.
Structured Exception Workflows
When an exclusion was flagged, the system routed the exception through a defined resolution workflow — requiring documentation, escalation, or waiver approval before the subcontractor could proceed.
Real-Time Compliance Dashboards
Project managers and risk teams gained live visibility into compliance status across the entire subcontractor roster — broken out by trade, project, and exception type.
